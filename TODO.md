### Standing rules

- **The trap that breaks a minor whenever it adds a column: the column must be nullable or defaulted.** A `NOT NULL` column with no default means the previous image's `INSERT`s fail against the new database, and by the mechanical test that quietly turns a minor into a **major**. It is the only way a minor accidentally violates its own versioning. `v1.6.0`'s two new tables (`credentials`, `push_subscriptions`), `v1.8.0`'s eight `applications` columns, and `v1.9.0`'s visa/hiring/timezone fields all shipped clean under it; the open `v1.11` batch adds no column at all, so the trap cannot fire here, but the rule stands for any future v1 minor. A column shipped loose under this rule is deferred, not forgotten: the `2.0.0` schema re-optimization item below owns the tightening pass, so the debt has an address.
- **Field admission test, for any new per-application column** (channel, agency, comp structure, language level, hiring entity, timezone, all of them): it must be **captured at prefill time by `UrlPrefillService`, or cost near-zero manual entry**. The app has one user, and a field he stops filling in after the fifth application is dead schema plus form friction. Nothing in the open plan adds a field, so this bites next on `2.0.0`'s `positions` entity and the tightening pass; it stays here as the standing test every future field is measured against.
- **Perishable-facts refresh rule, for every Japan-market item.** Each embeds perishable external facts: fee schedules, processing times, survey medians. A career tool that confidently states last year's rules is worse than one that says nothing, so each such item must state its **annual refresh cost** when it is scoped, and the sum of those lines is a real cap on how many of these a solo maintainer can ship. The open plan carries no market item (the visa/HSP research shipped with `v1.9.0`–`v1.10.0`); the rule governs the `2.0.0` career-intelligence item next, which already budgets this way ("one data-entry session a year").

### The centerpiece: rewrite to Hono + Nuxt on Cloudflare *(post-job; item one the moment the search succeeds)*

Decided 2026-07-22. What used to read as three scattered items in this cluster (a Nuxt "north star", a Hono backend, and a $0-hosting move off Railway) is **one move**, because it settles three bets at once and they share a single trigger:

- **Specialty signal (the portfolio bet).** Depth over breadth: rather than staying one more generic Next.js tracker, lean all the way into the framework the author already contributes to, so the flagship app *is* the flagship of a Vue/Nuxt-specialist-and-OSS-contributor identity.
- **$0 hosting (the cost bet).** Railway has **no free tier** (Hobby is a $5/month minimum), so today's ~$6.30/month (api ~$4.40 of it at a 0.44 GB average, measured 2026-07-20) only zeroes by leaving Railway entirely, *both* services. This rewrite lands both halves on Cloudflare's free tier by construction. Same trigger-shape as the Cloudflare-proxy revisit: the always-warm-demo rationale (the repo's job is Tokyo hiring signal; its first hit is a screener clicking the link) expires the moment the job lands, and cost wins.
- **Serverless modernization.** Cold starts stop mattering once nobody is being impressed by a live demo, so the trade-offs that would hurt a portfolio piece are free here.

The target stack, verified free as of 2026-07-22:

- **Backend: Rails → Hono on Cloudflare Workers.** ActiveRecord is replaced by a TS query layer (Drizzle or Kysely over `pg`), so the schema survives but the ORM does not. Solid Queue's persistent poller moves to Cloudflare Cron Triggers. The two invariants port as *shape*, not as a rewrite of the rules: `ApplicationFSM::TRANSITIONS` stays the single copy of the transition table (now in TS), and status changes still route through one transition service, never direct attribute writes.
- **Frontend: Next → Nuxt.** The single largest work item anywhere in this file: all of `web/`, the i18n navigation wrapper, the CSP-nonce `proxy.ts` guard, every component and form. Nuxt/Nitro's **native Cloudflare preset** makes the deploy trivial and is the cleaner of the two paths, versus keeping Next and shipping it through the OpenNext `@opennextjs/cloudflare` adapter.
- **Data: Postgres stays, the host changes.** Neon or Supabase free tier, fronted by **Cloudflare Hyperdrive** (connection pooler + query cache: free, no egress charge, `node-postgres` is its recommended driver, so the classic serverless connection-exhaustion problem is exactly what Hyperdrive absorbs). Two rejected paths recorded so they are not re-litigated: **D1 is SQLite, not Postgres**, so it is not a drop-in for this schema; and Cloudflare's *native* managed Postgres (via PlanetScale, billed through the Cloudflare account, June 2026) is **paid**, the convenience path, not the free one.
- **Public domain: `kk.chairulakmal.com`** (decided 2026-07-22), bound to the Worker as a custom-domain route. A subdomain of the personal apex on purpose: it puts the author's name as the primary information in the link, so a screener sees whose project this is before the page loads, the portfolio signal a standalone `karirkalyan.com` would throw away. It is also free and consistent with the $0 goal (one DNS record on a zone already run), and the terse `kk` is chosen over a self-documenting `karirkalyan.` slug precisely to keep the name the dominant token. **Prerequisite, and the one thing that inverts the signal:** `chairulakmal.com` itself must resolve to a real personal site or CV, because a screener will strip `kk.` to see who the author is, and a parked or 404 apex reads worse than no custom domain at all. A dedicated product domain is warranted only on a pivot to external users, which the north star rejects.

**Why v2 and not now:** the current search targets *any* role, so through it the Next app is the right call, it matches the most reqs and already demos; doing the rewrite mid-search would trade applying-time for a half-migrated flagship that demos worse than the working one. The moment the job lands, that calculus flips and this is item one. **What can start now anyway:** casual Nuxt/Vue OSS contributions carry the specialist signal on their own and need no rewrite, so they can begin anytime; only the flagship rewrite waits for the trigger. **Versioning:** by the mechanical test a rewrite that keeps the schema is not automatically a major, so this is not itself the `2.0.0`; it sits in the cluster because its trigger is the same date. The `positions` entity below is what forces the major.

- [ ] **`positions` (tenure) entity: the keystone; design it before building anything below.** *(`2.0.0`, the only major in this file)* Today `accepted` is a terminal status; a career tracker needs the job you then held as a first-class thing: company, title, start/end, and the comp structure `v1.8.0` shipped (normalized 年収). Comp history across positions (benchmarkable against the TokyoDev medians cited below), resume versioning, internal-promotion stages, and 職務経歴書 generation all hang off it: a 職務経歴書 is literally a positions table rendered as prose. Write it into SPEC.md's data model **first**; retrofitting it later means migrating what `accepted` means. **Scoping trigger: accepting an offer**, not finishing a prior release; the moment the search succeeds is the moment this entity is needed, or the app exits the user's life exactly when its retention story was supposed to begin.
- [ ] **Make the app survive its own success** *(post-`2.0.0`: every sub-item below hangs off `positions`)*. Today the lifecycle ends at `accepted`: the search closes and there is no reason to open the app again until the next one. Extend it into the tenure _between_ searches, so landing a job is a state change, not an exit:
  - **Resume as a living document**: the resume is already stored per application; add a current, versioned profile that keeps improving while employed, so the next application starts from the best version rather than a year-old PDF. Feeds the rirekisho/shokumu-keirekisho generation idea below.
  - **Low-noise market watch**: `wishlist` already models "interesting, not applied"; let it be the employed-mode default. Tracking relevant roles while happily employed is how you know your market value and when to move.
  - **Skills & certs against the market, not a wishlist**: `UrlPrefillService` already reads postings with Claude; extract the skills/certifications each tracked role asks for at prefill time, and the gap between what target roles want and what the profile holds becomes a ranked "learn next" list (JLPT level, cloud certs, a framework) with zero extra data entry. Goals can target the current employer too: an internal promotion has stages like an application does.
  - **Periodic check-ins**: Solid Queue and the mailer exist; a quarterly "update your resume, review your goals" nudge is one recurring job. Same dead-zone awareness as the calendar-aware follow-ups that shipped in `v1.4.0`.

  This repositions the tracker from a search tool you abandon on success into a career companion with a retention story: also the better portfolio argument, since every sub-item reuses machinery that already exists (bytea storage, `wishlist`, prefill extraction, recurring jobs) rather than demanding new infrastructure.

  **Scope boundary:** career growth does **not** mean drifting into a **networking CRM**, a **habit tracker**, or a **learning tracker**. Those are separate products with mature incumbents, and a broad-shallow app is a worse portfolio piece than a narrow-deep one. The admission test for any career-growth feature: **it must reuse the FSM, the timeline, the prefill extraction, or the `positions` entity**; if it needs none of them, it belongs in a different app. Concretely: contacts go no further than the recruiter-channel item's `agencies` table (that models submission ownership, not relationships); skills/certs/JLPT go no further than the gap list derived at prefill time (a ranked "learn next", never course progress or streaks); check-ins stay a recurring nudge, never a journaling habit loop.

- [ ] **Rirekisho + shokumu-keirekisho generation** *(deliberately parked past `2.0.0`; ship both together, after it)*. Japanese applications conventionally want two documents: 履歴書 (personal history, a standardised form) and 職務経歴書 (career history, free-form). Generating both as PDFs from stored profile data would be the clearest possible signal that the author understands the market. 履歴書 alone is a legal minor (additive, no schema break), but its twin *is* a `positions` table rendered as prose, and shipping half the pair, then maintaining a PDF toolchain for a year before the other half is even buildable, is the worst version of the trade. **Format question answered (2026-07-11):** the JIS template was indeed [withdrawn from the JIS standards in July 2020](https://japan-dev.com/blog/japanese-resume-rirekisho) (stationery stores still sell "JIS-compliant" pads, but it is a dead standard), and MHLW published a recommended replacement format (厚生労働省様式) designed for fair hiring: [gender optional; commute time, dependents, and spouse fields removed](https://www.gtalent.jp/blog/japanwork-en/job-hunting-en/rirekisho-en). **Target the MHLW format**: it is the closest thing to official, and its fair-hiring rationale is a good line in the README. **Carry cost (recorded 2026-07-11): the highest in this file.** PDF generation is a known maintenance sink (toolchain choice, CJK font embedding, layout drift), and on Cloudflare Workers (the centerpiece's runtime) it is further constrained to pure-JS/WASM libraries or an external rendering service, no native binary toolchain; the MHLW format itself can also churn. Highest wow, highest carry; scope it with both eyes open, the way the dark-mode decision (`CHANGELOG.md` § Decisions) weighed its cost side. **Storage of the pair is parked with it:** typed document slots for uploading a 履歴書/職務経歴書 alongside the English resume were considered for v1.x and deferred: more blobs on the pg_dump-backed database (the same cost the no-version-history decision refused) for documents whose real payoff arrives when generation ships. If uploads are wanted sooner, the shape is a typed `documents` table reusing the `v1.4.4` filename machinery, not more bytea columns.
